Have you ever just jumped in at the deep end? Without really knowing what to expect? Without thinking too much about what could go wrong? That's exactly how my new life feels right now.

Two months ago, I moved to Strasbourg — a city I had never seen before, in a country whose language I can barely speak. Why? Because of love. And perhaps also because I seem to always need a bit of chaos and adventure to feel alive.

## The first few days

The move itself was an adventure. Me with too much luggage on the FlixBus, on my way to an unknown life. I spent the first few days between furniture stores, supermarket aisles and trying to turn a flat into a home.

It was only after three days that I stood in front of the cathedral for the first time — and was overwhelmed. If I had known what seeing it would do to me, I would have gone there straight after I arrived.

> It reminds me of home, and at the same time that I am now somewhere else.

## A contradiction that works

Strasbourg is many things to me at once. Historical and modern, hectic and quiet, French and German, familiar and yet foreign. I love the narrow streets, the old houses with their crooked beams, the light that falls through the windows of the old town in the evening.

But there are also those other moments — when I'm sitting on the tram and don't understand the conversations around me. When someone asks me something and I just smile helplessly because I don't know the words.

I love language, I love being able to express myself precisely and well. In moments like these, I realise how much I miss that.

## When does a city become home?

I now have a regular jogging route, I know which tram line takes me home, and where to find good tarte flambée. I have new favourite places, new and old habits in new places — and sometimes I ask myself: is that enough to say you've arrived?

Maybe arriving isn't a moment at all, but a process. One in which you learn that you can't control everything. That uncertainty is part of it. That courage often means carrying on, even when you feel lost.

Strasbourg is still new to me, but perhaps that's exactly what's so wonderful about it. It's the city where I'm learning to reinvent myself.
